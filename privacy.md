# RepQuest privacy policy

_Last updated 24 November 2026_

## The short version

RepQuest is a workout log with a game, made by MujarStudios in the Philippines. It works without an account, and everything you log stays on your phone unless you choose to back it up. There are no ads, no analytics in the app and no tracking across other apps or websites, and we never sell your data or use it for advertising.

A few things do leave your phone. To check whether you have RepQuest Pro, the app asks RevenueCat, our subscription provider, which knows you by a random identifier, or your account’s if you are signed in. If you create an account and agree to back up your data, your email address and a copy of your data are stored in our cloud database in the European Union. If you make or join a party, a small board shared with friends, its members see your hero’s name, your rewarded workouts this week, your weeks on plan in a row and your hero’s best stage and, once you agree to show them, the title it wears and the flag of the country or region you chose. If you join the leaderboards, every player with an account can see your hero’s name, its title, that flag, your rewarded workouts this week, your hero’s best stage and its level. If you turn on your hero page, your friends (and, if you choose, the players who see you on a board) can see your hero as it looks, its name, title and flag, its level, Training Rank and best stage, your rewarded workouts this week and what you choose to show on it. If you buy RepQuest Pro, the store takes the payment and tells us about the sale. So that free days come once per phone, the app sends our cloud database a one-way code made from your phone, never tied to you, your account or what you log. While you track a run, walk or ride with the real map on, the app downloads the map around you from OpenFreeMap, which can tell from it roughly where you are, never your route. Once you have accepted the terms, the app also asks Expo whether there is a newer version of its code and pictures, giving it a random identifier made on your phone. Each is explained below, with your rights and how to reach us.

If you are under 18, ask a parent or guardian to read this with you. What you log stays on your phone, and nothing is backed up unless you make an account and tick the box that agrees to it. Nothing is shown to other players unless you join a party or the leaderboards, or turn on a hero page, each behind a box of its own.

## Who we are and how to reach us

RepQuest is made by MujarStudios, a developer based in the Philippines (“we”). We decide how and why personal data is used in RepQuest, so we are responsible for it: its “controller”, or “personal information controller” under Philippine law.

For anything about your privacy, including the rights described below, write to mujarstudios@gmail.com. The person who answers there is our data protection officer under Philippine law, and our privacy contact under the other laws named in “Rights where you live”. Our postal address is shown on RepQuest’s page in Google Play, and we will give it to anyone who asks.

## What stays on your phone

Your profile (hero name, units, weekly plan and, if you enter them, your body weight and your height), the answers you gave when you started (your goals, where you train and how experienced you are), your exercises, routines and workouts (with any notes and effort ratings), your hero and their game progress, and your settings. It is kept in a database inside the app, protected by your phone’s app sandbox and lock screen. We cannot see it unless you back it up or send it to us.

If you choose a country or region (Settings → Profile & plan), its flag is shown beside your hero’s name. It is your choice alone: the list may offer the region your phone is set to as a suggestion, read on your phone only, and nothing is kept until you pick one yourself. The app never uses your location or your network to work out where you are for this, and you can take it off again in the same place. It is kept with your settings, and is part of the cloud backup if you have one. Other players see the flag only on a board you have joined yourself, a party’s or the leaderboards, or on your hero page if you turn it on, as the sections on those say.

When you accept the terms and conditions, the app notes which version you accepted and when. The note stays on your phone, like the data above, and is not part of the cloud backup. So that the first screens are drawn quickly, it also notes how your hero looks, your training hall and the Ossuary zone you last played, outside the database, and it keeps your chosen theme there too. Erase all data forgets all of these notes; removing an account’s data from the phone forgets the notes about its hero.

On an iPhone, the phone’s own backup (to iCloud, if you turn it on, or to a computer) can include this database, the notes above and the error log below, as it does for other apps. On Android, the phone’s own backup, to your Google account or straight to a new phone, leaves all of these out: it takes only small settings that parts of the app keep for themselves, such as the reminders and rest alerts scheduled on the phone, the identifier RevenueCat knows you by with its copy of your subscription’s status, and the random identifier the app gives Expo when it checks for updates. So on Android, a guest’s history does not move to a new phone by itself; an account’s cloud backup does. A phone’s own backup belongs to your Apple or Google account, under their terms; we cannot read it.

If the app hits an error, a short technical log is kept on your phone: the error messages, where in the app they happened, when, and the app and system versions, with email addresses and sign-in tokens removed. It is sent only if you share it yourself from Settings → Help & tutorials, where you can also clear it.

Now and then, after you finish something, the app may ask the store to show its rating prompt, and it notes on your phone when it last asked. Whether you rate RepQuest, and what you write, is between you and the store.

## Location and steps

If you track a run, walk, ride or hike, the app uses your phone’s location while the tracking screen is open, to measure the distance and draw your route. It never uses your location at any other time, and never in the background. The route is kept on your phone and the app never uploads it (an iPhone’s own backup can include it, as above). It leaves your phone only in a workout image or a data export you make yourself, and it is deleted with its workout.

With the real map on (Settings → Workouts → Real map while tracking, on unless you turn it off), the tracking screen also shows the streets, parks and water around you. To draw them, the app downloads squares of map from OpenFreeMap, a free map service run by Hyperknot Software Kft. in Hungary, with map data from OpenStreetMap. Each download asks for one square about 2.4 km across (smaller away from the equator), so OpenFreeMap can tell from it roughly where you are, and it receives your phone’s IP address, as any download does. It is an independent service with its own privacy policy, which says it keeps no IP addresses in its access logs. The app never sends it your route, your exact position, your account or anything else about you, and keeps the squares only in its memory while it runs. With the real map off, the app downloads no map and draws your route on the game’s own map art.

While you track a walk, run or hike, the app also uses your location to let you pick up finds: small rewards for your hero. Some are placed on footpaths and in parks near you and picked up when you come close, and one more comes with each kilometre you cover. Where the placed ones are is worked out on your phone, from the map squares it has already downloaded. Nothing more is downloaded or sent for finds. The app keeps when you picked each find up and the essence it gave your hero, never where a find was or where you were; that record, with no place in it, is part of your hero’s progress, and of its cloud backup if you have one.

If you turn on step counting, the app reads your steps from your phone’s motion sensor (on iPhone, the day’s count from Motion & Fitness) to show your daily total and the steps in each activity. On Android, if you also allow it, the app reads the day’s step count from Health Connect, where your phone or apps such as Google Fit record steps, so that the steps you take while RepQuest is closed count too. It reads only the step count, never writes to Health Connect, and uses what it reads only in the app, to show your steps and count your day streak, as Google Play’s Health Connect policy requires, including its limited-use rules: never sold, shared or used for advertising. Step counts stay on your phone, apart from a workout image or a data export you make yourself. So that your day streak can count them, the app keeps on your phone the days your steps reached your daily step goal: the date only, never the count. To find those days it also reads the daily counts of the past week that it has not checked yet (from Motion & Fitness on iPhone, or from Health Connect if you allow it), never from before you made your hero, and keeps only which days reached your goal. They are not part of the cloud backup and are never sent; they leave your phone only in a data export you make yourself.

## Workout images and photos

A workout image (its numbers, your hero, which can show its name, level, the title it wears, its companion and the flag of the country or region you chose, and, for a tracked activity, its route) or a month image (the month’s numbers, its best lifts and your hero) is made on your phone only when you ask for one, and goes only where you choose to share it. By default a route on it leaves off the start and end, which are often near home.

To put a photo behind the image, you can choose one from your photos or take one with the camera. The app asks to use the camera only when you choose to take a photo, and sees only the photo you choose. It uses the photo on your phone to make the image and never uploads it. It keeps no copy of the photo apart from the image you share, which stays in the app’s cache until you share another one or erase your data.

## Optional cloud backup

If you create an account to back up your progress, we store your email address, to send you sign-in codes and to know your account, and a copy of your profile, answers, exercises, routines, workouts, hero and settings (with the country or region you chose, if you chose one), so that you can restore them or use them on another phone. Routes, step counts and settings that only make sense on one phone (such as volume, reminders and the look of your workout images) stay on that phone. The backup also holds a random code for each of your phones, so that it can tell them apart.

The copy is stored with Supabase, our database and sign-in provider, in its data centre in the European Union (Ireland). Each account can reach only its own data. As the service’s administrators we can technically reach it too, and we do so only to run, fix or protect the service, to answer a request from you, or when the law requires it.

Sign-in codes (and the code that confirms it’s you before your account is deleted) are emailed to you through our email provider, currently Google’s Gmail service, which keeps a copy of each email sent (your address and the code) in our email account; we delete those copies within 30 days. Like any online service, Supabase also records technical details of each sign-in and request, such as your email address or IP address, the time and your phone’s software, to run and secure the service.

## Parties

A party is a small board you can share with friends. It is off until you make or join one, and each time the app first asks you to tick a box that says what will be shown. A party needs an account, because the board has to know which member is you.

While you are in a party, the app sends our cloud database your hero’s name and three numbers your phone works out: your rewarded workouts this week (never more than 7), your weeks on plan in a row, and your hero’s best stage in the game. The title your hero wears and the country or region you chose (each only if there is one) are sent only while Show my title and flag to my parties is on. That switch is on the Parties screen. It comes on when you tick the box, which names both, for a party while you are in no other; otherwise it is off until you switch it on, and you can switch it off again at any time, which takes both off your parties’ boards. It sends them when you make or join a party, when you open the boards and when they change. Nothing else from your log is sent for a party: no exercises, weights, body weight, height, steps, routes or places.

The members of a party see each other’s hero name and those numbers, and the title and flag of a member who agreed to show them, and nothing else: never your email address or your account. Anyone who has a party’s code can join it and see its board, so share a code only with people you know; the party’s owner can remove a member and give the party a new code. A hero’s name is typed by its player: the same is taken out of it as on a leaderboard, and a name that holds a word on our list of words that are not allowed, or would pass for RepQuest or its staff, is shown as “Hero”. To report or block a member, use their row on the board, as the section on reports and blocks says. A member you block keeps their row, shown to you as a blocked player with no title or flag.

Your row is stored with Supabase, beside your account, until you leave the party, until its owner removes you, or until you delete your account, which takes you out of every party; a party with no members is deleted. We also remove a member whose app has not updated their row for a year. On your phone the app keeps whether you are in a party; whether you agreed to show your title and flag is kept with your settings, and is part of the cloud backup.

## Leaderboards

Leaderboards are boards that every RepQuest player with an account can read. You are not on them until you join, and each time the app first asks you to tick a box that says what will be shown. Joining needs an account, because a board has to know which entry is yours. Reading needs one too, because our server answers only players who are signed in.

Once you have joined, the app sends our cloud database your hero’s name, the title it wears and the country or region you chose (each only if there is one), and three numbers your phone works out: your rewarded workouts this week (never more than 4, the most a leaderboard counts), your hero’s best stage in the game and its level. It sends them when you join, when you open the leaderboards and when they change. Your entry keeps your count for the week before too: weeks start on each player’s own Monday, and a player whose week has not turned yet is shown that one. Our server holds each number to what the game allows, takes emoji, other pictures and characters that cannot be seen out of a name, shows as “Hero” a name that holds a word on our list of words that are not allowed or would pass for RepQuest or its staff, and checks nothing more. Nothing else from your log is sent for a leaderboard: no exercises, weights, body weight, height, steps, routes or places.

Every player with an account can see your entry: your hero’s name, its title, your flag, those numbers and your place on each board. They never see your email address or your account: another player’s app knows your entry only by a code made for the leaderboards. A hero’s name is typed by its player and is public here, so do not put your real name or anything private in it. To report or block a player, use their entry’s row, as the section on reports and blocks says. Hide on this phone, beside it, takes an entry off the boards your own phone shows.

When you read a board, the app tells our server which board you asked for and which week your phone is in and, for the board of your flag, the country or region you chose. None of that is kept. The board of your friends shows your friends and the members of your parties who joined the leaderboards too.

Your entry is stored with Supabase, beside your account, until you leave the leaderboards or delete your account: either deletes it at once. We also delete an entry whose app has not updated it for a year. We can hide an entry that breaks the terms from other players. If we do, the app tells you why, and a note that the entry is hidden, with our reason, stays with your account until we lift it or the account is deleted. On your phone the app keeps whether you have joined and which entries you hid on that phone, by the codes made for the leaderboards; that list never leaves the phone.

## Hero pages and friends

A hero page is a page about your hero that other players can open. It is off until you turn it on, and each time the app first asks you to tick a box that says what it shows. It needs an account, as a page has to belong to one.

While your page is on, the app sends our cloud database what it shows: your hero’s name, the title it wears and the country or region you chose (each only if there is one); how your hero looks, as codes for its body, skin, hair, eyes, face, build, outfit, weapon, helmet and the piece it wears at its neck; its companion; the training hall, pose and motto you choose for the page (a motto is a line from the app’s own list); and numbers your phone works out: its level, its Training Rank, its best stage in the game, your rewarded workouts this week (never more than 4), how many achievements and looks you have, and up to three achievements you pick. Your workouts logged in all and your weeks on plan in a row, now and at best, are sent only while Show my training numbers is on: it is off until you switch it on, and off again whenever you turn the page off. The app sends your page when you turn it on, each time the app starts and when what it shows changes. Nothing else from your log is sent for it: no exercises, weights, body weight, height, steps, routes, places or your day streak.

Your friends can open your page. If you switch on Players who see me can open it, so can any player with an account who can see you on a leaderboard you joined or in a party you share, and they can send you a friend request from it. Nobody else can open it, nobody can find it by searching for a name, and nobody you block, or who blocks you, can open it. Players never see your email address or your account: another player’s app knows your page only by a code made for it. Your page also has a friend code, which you give to whom you choose; anyone who has it can send you a friend request. While a request waits, the player who sent it sees your hero’s name, the title it wears and your flag, and you see theirs, with its level and best stage.

Your friends and the requests you send and receive are stored with Supabase, beside your account: who asked whom, and when. A request is deleted when it is answered or withdrawn; one not answered within 14 days lapses, and is deleted at our server’s next clean-up. So that a request cannot be sent again and again, our server keeps for 30 days when you last asked each player, or when a friend removed you. A friendship lasts until either of you removes the other, blocks the other, turns their page off or deletes their account; neither is sent a message about it.

Your page is stored with Supabase until you turn it off or delete your account: either deletes it at once, with your friends and your requests. We also delete a page whose app has not updated it for a year. We can hide a page that breaks the terms from other players. If we do, the app tells you why, and a note that the page is hidden, with our reason, stays with your account until we lift it or the account is deleted. What you chose for your page (whether it shows your training numbers, its hall, pose, motto and achievements) is kept with your settings, and is part of the cloud backup; on your phone the app also keeps whether your page is on.

## Reports and blocks

To report a player, you choose one reason from a list, on their row on a party’s board or a leaderboard, their hero page or their friend request. The app sends our cloud database the reason and the code of what you reported (an entry, a membership, a page or a request), and our server finds from it the player’s account and the name it shows. A report holds no words of your own, and the player is never told who reported them. We keep your report, with your account, for 30 days, and it is deleted at our server’s next clean-up after that. Where the app cannot send reports yet, Report opens an email to us that you send yourself.

A name that is reported is kept as a case: the account, the name, its state and, once we decide, our reason and a note of our own. A case whose reports are all gone is deleted, unless we decided it: a name we upheld is kept while its account is, so that it stays “Hero”, and one we cleared is kept for a year after, so that reports do not hide it again. When three players, each with an account at least a week old, report a name itself, our server shows every name of that player as “Hero” to other players, by itself, while those reports are kept (at most 30 days unless we decide sooner), and does not tell that player.

When you block a player, our server keeps your block (the account you blocked, the name you saw, where you blocked them and when) until you lift it or either account is deleted. It is never shown to the player you blocked. While it stands, our server leaves their entry out of the boards you read, shows their row on a party’s board you share as a blocked player, and keeps hero pages and friend requests from passing between you.

Our server also keeps, with your account, how many reports and blocks you made in the last hour, so that they cannot be used to flood it, and deletes that count a day later at its next clean-up.

## Subscriptions

RepQuest Pro is bought through Google Play or the App Store, which takes the payment under its own privacy policy. The app never sees your card or bank details.

To know whether you have Pro, the app asks RevenueCat, our subscription provider, when it starts and when you come back to it, whether or not you have subscribed. It first asks once you have accepted the terms. RevenueCat knows you by an identifier that is either random, made on your phone, or, if you are signed in, your account’s identifier, never your email address. With each request it also receives technical details, such as your IP address, your phone’s system and app version, your language and your store’s country. That identifier is the only one the app gives it: no advertising identifier, and nothing that identifies your phone’s hardware.

If you subscribe, RevenueCat also receives your purchases of RepQuest Pro from the store (what you bought, when, its price and currency, the store’s receipt for it, and when it renews or ends). If you sign in after subscribing, the purchase moves to your account’s identifier. RevenueCat uses this only to check your subscription and to report subscriptions to us. Its code in the app keeps your subscription’s status on your phone, so that Pro works offline.

If you are signed in, your subscription’s status (the plan, the store, when it renews or ends, and whether a payment failed) is also stored with your account in the app’s cloud database.

Google Play sends us a report of each sale: its order number, date, product, price and tax, the phone model, and the buyer’s country and, for some sales, their state or postal code. The App Store sends us sales totals by country. Neither includes your name, email address or card details. We use these reports for our accounts and taxes, and to help with refunds.

A new hero’s 14 free days come once per phone. So that a new hero, a new account or a reinstall does not start them again, the app sends our cloud database a one-way code made from an identifier your phone gives RepQuest alone (on Android, the identifier Android gives this app; on an iPhone, a random one the app keeps in your phone’s keychain), and says whether a hero exists yet. The code cannot be turned back into that identifier, and it is never tied to your account, your hero or anything you log. Our server keeps the code with when the phone first had free days and when it was last seen, and tells the app those dates and its own time, which the app uses to check your phone’s clock. The app first asks once you have accepted the terms, and before a hero is made it only looks. With each request our provider also receives your IP address, as with any connection. Our server takes only so many new phones an hour. So that one network cannot take all of that room, it also counts how many new phones’ codes come from each internet address in an hour (an IPv6 address by its first half, which a home or a phone usually has to itself). It keeps that count under a hash of the address and the hour rather than the address itself, but an address can be found from its hash by trying addresses, so we treat it as the address. The count is never kept with a phone’s code, an account or anything you log, and it is deleted once its hour is over, at our server’s next check of any phone.

Whether the free days, or a tester’s free year, have run out is then worked out on your phone. A hero made since 9 November 2026 also keeps, with its own data (and its backup, if you have one), the date its phone first had free days.

## App updates

Google Play and the App Store deliver new versions of RepQuest. The app can also download small updates to its own code and pictures directly from Expo (650 Industries, Inc., in the United States), whose tools it is built with.

Once you have accepted the terms, the app asks Expo each time it starts whether there is an update for it. With that request Expo receives a random identifier made on your phone, never tied to you, your account or anything you log, your phone’s system (Android or iPhone), the version of the app and of any update it is running, and your IP address, as with any connection. Nothing you log is sent. Expo keeps its records of these requests under its own privacy policy. The app has no setting that turns this check off; removing the app ends it.

## Notifications

Rest timer alerts and training reminders are scheduled on your phone and only if you turn them on. No push notification service is used.

## Emails you send us

If you write to us, or send us an error report, we receive your email address and what you send, in our email account with Google (Gmail). We use it only to answer you and to fix problems. We keep it for up to two years after our last reply, and longer only if a legal claim needs it.

## Health data

Some of what you log can count as health data, or other sensitive data, in some countries: your workouts, body weight, height, steps, goals such as fat loss or health, and how experienced you are. From your phone’s sensors the app reads only your location while you track an activity, your steps if you turn on step counting (on Android, also the daily step counts of today and the past week from Health Connect if you allow it), and the camera when you take a photo for a workout image. It reads nothing else from Health Connect, writes nothing to it, and does not read Apple Health or any other health data.

All of this stays on your phone, unless you back up your workouts, body weight, goals and experience by creating an account and agreeing to it, or put some of it in a workout image or a data export you share yourself; the app never uploads routes, steps or your height, which is never part of the backup. If you join a party, your rewarded workouts this week and your weeks on plan in a row are shown to its members, with your agreement. If you join the leaderboards, your rewarded workouts this week are shown to other players, with your agreement. If you turn on your hero page, your rewarded workouts this week, and while you show them your workouts logged in all and your weeks on plan in a row, are shown to your friends and, if you choose, the players who see you, with your agreement. Your height and body weight are used only on your phone: to show your BMI and what training builds at your size, and, when a starter plan is made with both, to adjust its exercises by your BMI. We never use any of it for advertising, marketing or data mining, and never sell it.

## Why we use your data, and on what grounds

Everything we receive comes from you, from your phone, or from the stores and RevenueCat as described above. The stores also show us download numbers and, from phones whose owners chose to share diagnostics with app developers, crash reports without names or email addresses. We get nothing else about you from other companies, and we use what we receive only for the purposes below. The laws of many countries, such as the GDPR, ask us to name the legal ground for each.

Running the cloud backup and signing you in, checking and recording your RepQuest Pro subscription, and downloading the map around you while you track an activity with the real map on: because it is needed to provide what you asked for under our terms (contract).

Backing up the data above that can count as health data: your explicit consent, which you give by ticking the box that names this data before the app sends you a sign-in code. It is separate from accepting the terms, and without it the app makes no account and backs nothing up. You can withdraw it at any time by deleting your account in Settings → Account & backup, which deletes the backup, without affecting what was done before.

Showing your hero’s name and numbers to the members of a party: your explicit consent, which you give by ticking the box that names them each time you make or join a party. It is separate from accepting the terms and from the backup. You can withdraw it at any time by leaving the party, which removes your row from its board, without affecting what was shown before. Showing your hero’s title and your flag to your parties: your explicit consent, which you give by that box for a party made or joined while you are in no other, or by switching Show my title and flag to my parties on. You can withdraw it at any time by switching it off, which takes both off the boards.

Showing your entry on the leaderboards: your explicit consent, which you give by ticking the box that names what is shown each time you join. It is separate from accepting the terms, from the backup and from a party. You can withdraw it at any time by leaving the leaderboards, which deletes your entry, without affecting what was shown before.

Showing your hero page to your friends: your explicit consent, which you give by ticking the box that names what it shows each time you turn it on. It is separate from accepting the terms, from the backup, from a party and from the leaderboards. Showing your training numbers on it, and letting the players who see you open it: your explicit consent too, which you give by switching each on. You can withdraw any of these at any time, without affecting what was shown before: switch it off, or turn the page off, which deletes it.

Remembering which phones have had free days, counting new phones by the internet address they come from, and checking the phone’s clock: our legitimate interest in offering free days fairly, once per phone, and preventing abuse of the free offer. The code is one-way and holds nothing else, and the count by address is kept under a hash, so the impact on you is small, and it does not override your rights.

Keeping reports, cases and blocks, showing as “Hero” a name three players reported or that holds a word on our list, and hiding what breaks the terms: our legitimate interest in keeping players safe from abuse, which does not override your rights. Keeping RepQuest secure and up to date, preventing abuse, fixing problems, answering your messages and defending legal claims: our legitimate interest in running a safe, working service, which does not override your rights. Keeping records the law requires, such as for tax, and answering lawful requests from authorities: a legal obligation.

You do not have to give us any personal data to use RepQuest. An email address is needed only for cloud backup, and a store account only to buy RepQuest Pro. The app makes no automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects. Two fixed rules are worked out on your phone: the end of the free days, once per phone, and, if you give your height and weight, the change your BMI makes to a starter plan’s exercises, which the plan shows you and you can change. Your BMI is never sent to us. We do not use your data to train artificial intelligence models.

## How long data is kept

On your phone: until you delete it, erase your data or remove the app. In the cloud backup: until you delete your account, or until we delete an account unused for two years, after warning you by email at least 30 days before, as the terms say. The backup’s working records (a copy of a change that clashed with another phone’s, and the list of changes already received, which keeps a change sent twice from counting twice) are deleted after 30 days. Your data is then deleted from our cloud database at once, and our provider’s records of sign-ins and requests, and the copies in its backups, are deleted as they expire, within 30 days.

At RevenueCat: a signed-in account’s purchase record is deleted when you delete the account. A record made without an account, which lets a purchase made on that phone be restored, is deleted once it has gone unused for 12 months with no subscription running. The stores keep their own records of your purchases. We keep the stores’ sales reports, and other records the law requires, such as for tax, for as long as that law requires. Sign-in code emails are deleted from our email account within 30 days, and messages from you are kept as the section on emails says.

Free-days codes: kept with their two dates until two years after the phone was last seen, then deleted. The count of new phones from one internet address is deleted once its hour is over, at our server’s next check of any phone. On your phone, the date its free days began and the time check are kept until you remove the app, and the time our server last said it was too busy to take a new phone is kept until a check goes through.

Parties: your row on a board is deleted when you leave the party, are removed from it or delete your account, and a party with no members is deleted. A member whose app has not updated their row for a year is removed. The count of your tries to make or join a party in the last hour is kept with your account, starts again each hour, and is deleted a day later at our server’s next clean-up.

Leaderboards: your entry is deleted when you leave the leaderboards or delete your account, and an entry whose app has not updated it for a year is deleted. A note that we have hidden an entry, with our reason, is kept with the account until we lift it or the account is deleted. The count of what your app sent to and asked of the leaderboards in the last hour is kept with your account, starts again each hour, and is deleted a day later at our server’s next clean-up.

Hero pages: your page is deleted when you turn it off or delete your account, with your friends and your requests, and a page whose app has not updated it for a year is deleted. A request lapses after 14 days, and the note of when you last asked a player after 30. A note that we have hidden a page, with our reason, is kept with the account until we lift it or the account is deleted. The count of what your app sent to and asked of hero pages in the last hour is kept with your account, and deleted a day later at our server’s next clean-up.

Reports and blocks: a report is deleted 30 days after it was made, a case as the section on reports and blocks says, and a block when it is lifted or either account is deleted; the count of the last hour’s reports and blocks a day later. All of them go with your account when you delete it.

## How we protect your data

Data on your phone is protected by its app sandbox and lock screen, your sign-in is kept in the phone’s secure storage, and RepQuest uses no passwords. Data that leaves your phone travels encrypted (HTTPS), our providers encrypt the data they store, and each account can reach only its own records in the cloud database. Card details never reach us.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach puts your data at risk, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.
