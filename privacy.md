# RepQuest privacy policy

_Last updated 10 November 2026_

## The short version

RepQuest is a workout log with a game, made by MujarStudios in the Philippines. It works without an account, and everything you log stays on your phone unless you choose to back it up. There are no ads, no analytics in the app and no tracking across other apps or websites, and we never sell your data or use it for advertising.

A few things do leave your phone. To check whether you have RepQuest Pro, the app asks RevenueCat, our subscription provider, which knows you by a random identifier, or your account’s if you are signed in. If you create an account and agree to back up your data, your email address and a copy of your data are stored in our cloud database in the European Union. If you buy RepQuest Pro, the store takes the payment and tells us about the sale. So that free days come once per phone, the app sends our cloud database a one-way code made from your phone, never tied to you, your account or what you log. While you track a run, walk or ride with the real map on, the app downloads the map around you from OpenFreeMap, which can tell from it roughly where you are, never your route. Once you have accepted the terms, the app also asks Expo whether there is a newer version of its code and pictures, giving it a random identifier made on your phone. Each is explained below, with your rights and how to reach us.

If you are under 18, ask a parent or guardian to read this with you. What you log stays on your phone, and nothing is backed up unless you make an account and tick the box that agrees to it.

## Who we are and how to reach us

RepQuest is made by MujarStudios, a developer based in the Philippines (“we”). We decide how and why personal data is used in RepQuest, so we are responsible for it: its “controller”, or “personal information controller” under Philippine law.

For anything about your privacy, including the rights described below, write to mujarstudios@gmail.com. The person who answers there is our data protection officer under Philippine law, and our privacy contact under the other laws named in “Rights where you live”. Our postal address is shown on RepQuest’s page in Google Play, and we will give it to anyone who asks.

## What stays on your phone

Your profile (hero name, units, weekly plan and, if you enter them, your body weight and your height), the answers you gave when you started (your goals, where you train and how experienced you are), your exercises, routines and workouts (with any notes and effort ratings), your hero and their game progress, and your settings. It is kept in a database inside the app, protected by your phone’s app sandbox and lock screen. We cannot see it unless you back it up or send it to us.

When you accept the terms and conditions, the app notes which version you accepted and when. The note stays on your phone, like the data above, and is not part of the cloud backup. So that the first screens are drawn quickly, it also notes how your hero looks, your training hall and the Ossuary zone you last played, outside the database, and it keeps your chosen theme there too. Erase all data forgets all of these notes; removing an account’s data from the phone forgets the notes about its hero.

If you turn on your phone’s own backup (to your Google account or iCloud), it can include this database, the notes above and the error log below, as it does for other apps. That backup belongs to your account, under Google’s or Apple’s terms; we cannot read it.

If the app hits an error, a short technical log is kept on your phone: the error messages, where in the app they happened, when, and the app and system versions, with email addresses and sign-in tokens removed. It is sent only if you share it yourself from Settings → Help & tutorials, where you can also clear it.

Now and then, after you finish something, the app may ask the store to show its rating prompt, and it notes on your phone when it last asked. Whether you rate RepQuest, and what you write, is between you and the store.

## Location and steps

If you track a run, walk, ride or hike, the app uses your phone’s location while the tracking screen is open, to measure the distance and draw your route. It never uses your location at any other time, and never in the background. The route is kept on your phone and the app never uploads it (your phone’s own backup can include it, as above). It leaves your phone only in a workout image or a data export you make yourself, and it is deleted with its workout.

With the real map on (Settings → Workouts → Real map while tracking, on unless you turn it off), the tracking screen also shows the streets, parks and water around you. To draw them, the app downloads squares of map from OpenFreeMap, a free map service run by Hyperknot Software Kft. in Hungary, with map data from OpenStreetMap. Each download asks for one square about 2.4 km across (smaller away from the equator), so OpenFreeMap can tell from it roughly where you are, and it receives your phone’s IP address, as any download does. It is an independent service with its own privacy policy, which says it keeps no IP addresses in its access logs. The app never sends it your route, your exact position, your account or anything else about you, and keeps the squares only in its memory while it runs. With the real map off, the app downloads no map and draws your route on the game’s own map art.

If you turn on step counting, the app reads your steps from your phone’s motion sensor (on iPhone, the day’s count from Motion & Fitness) to show your daily total and the steps in each activity. Step counts stay on your phone, apart from a workout image or a data export you make yourself.

## Workout images and photos

A workout image (its numbers, your hero, which can show its name, level and companion, and, for a tracked activity, its route) or a month image (the month’s numbers, its best lifts and your hero) is made on your phone only when you ask for one, and goes only where you choose to share it. By default a route on it leaves off the start and end, which are often near home.

To put a photo behind the image, you can choose one from your photos or take one with the camera. The app asks to use the camera only when you choose to take a photo, and sees only the photo you choose. It uses the photo on your phone to make the image and never uploads it. It keeps no copy of the photo apart from the image you share, which stays in the app’s cache until you share another one or erase your data.

## Optional cloud backup

If you create an account to back up your progress, we store your email address, to send you sign-in codes and to know your account, and a copy of your profile, answers, exercises, routines, workouts, hero and settings, so that you can restore them or use them on another phone. Routes, step counts and settings that only make sense on one phone (such as volume, reminders and the look of your workout images) stay on that phone. The backup also holds a random code for each of your phones, so that it can tell them apart.

The copy is stored with Supabase, our database and sign-in provider, in its data centre in the European Union (Ireland). Each account can reach only its own data. As the service’s administrators we can technically reach it too, and we do so only to run, fix or protect the service, to answer a request from you, or when the law requires it.

Sign-in codes (and the code that confirms it’s you before your account is deleted) are emailed to you through our email provider, currently Google’s Gmail service, which keeps a copy of each email sent (your address and the code) in our email account; we delete those copies within 30 days. Like any online service, Supabase also records technical details of each sign-in and request, such as your email address or IP address, the time and your phone’s software, to run and secure the service.

## Subscriptions

RepQuest Pro is bought through Google Play or the App Store, which takes the payment under its own privacy policy. The app never sees your card or bank details.

To know whether you have Pro, the app asks RevenueCat, our subscription provider, when it starts and when you come back to it, whether or not you have subscribed. It first asks once you have accepted the terms. RevenueCat knows you by an identifier that is either random, made on your phone, or, if you are signed in, your account’s identifier, never your email address. With each request it also receives technical details, such as your IP address, your phone’s system and app version, your language and your store’s country. That identifier is the only one the app gives it: no advertising identifier, and nothing that identifies your phone’s hardware.

If you subscribe, RevenueCat also receives your purchases of RepQuest Pro from the store (what you bought, when, its price and currency, the store’s receipt for it, and when it renews or ends). If you sign in after subscribing, the purchase moves to your account’s identifier. RevenueCat uses this only to check your subscription and to report subscriptions to us. Its code in the app keeps your subscription’s status on your phone, so that Pro works offline.

If you are signed in, your subscription’s status (the plan, the store, when it renews or ends, and whether a payment failed) is also stored with your account in the app’s cloud database.

Google Play sends us a report of each sale: its order number, date, product, price and tax, the phone model, and the buyer’s country and, for some sales, their state or postal code. The App Store sends us sales totals by country. Neither includes your name, email address or card details. We use these reports for our accounts and taxes, and to help with refunds.

A new hero’s 14 free days come once per phone. So that a new hero, a new account or a reinstall does not start them again, the app sends our cloud database a one-way code made from an identifier your phone gives RepQuest alone (on Android, the identifier Android gives this app; on an iPhone, a random one the app keeps in your phone’s keychain), and says whether a hero exists yet. The code cannot be turned back into that identifier, and it is never tied to your account, your hero or anything you log. Our server keeps the code with when the phone first had free days and when it was last seen, and tells the app those dates and its own time, which the app uses to check your phone’s clock. The app first asks once you have accepted the terms, and before a hero is made it only looks. With each request our provider also receives your IP address, as with any connection.

Whether the free days, or a tester’s free year, have run out is then worked out on your phone. A hero made since 9 November 2026 also keeps, with its own data (and its backup, if you have one), the date its phone first had free days.

## App updates

Google Play and the App Store deliver new versions of RepQuest. The app can also download small updates to its own code and pictures directly from Expo (650 Industries, Inc., in the United States), whose tools it is built with.

Once you have accepted the terms, the app asks Expo each time it starts whether there is an update for it. With that request Expo receives a random identifier made on your phone, never tied to you, your account or anything you log, your phone’s system (Android or iPhone), the version of the app and of any update it is running, and your IP address, as with any connection. Nothing you log is sent. Expo keeps its records of these requests under its own privacy policy. The app has no setting that turns this check off; removing the app ends it.

## Notifications

Rest timer alerts and training reminders are scheduled on your phone and only if you turn them on. No push notification service is used.

## Emails you send us

If you write to us, or send us an error report, we receive your email address and what you send, in our email account with Google (Gmail). We use it only to answer you and to fix problems. We keep it for up to two years after our last reply, and longer only if a legal claim needs it.

## Health data

Some of what you log can count as health data, or other sensitive data, in some countries: your workouts, body weight, height, steps, goals such as fat loss or health, and how experienced you are. From your phone’s sensors the app reads only your location while you track an activity, your steps if you turn on step counting, and the camera when you take a photo for a workout image. It does not read Apple Health, Health Connect or any other health data.

All of this stays on your phone, unless you back up your workouts, body weight, goals and experience by creating an account and agreeing to it, or put some of it in a workout image or a data export you share yourself; the app never uploads routes, steps or your height, which is never part of the backup. Your height and body weight are used only to show your BMI and what training builds at your size, on your phone. We never use any of it for advertising, marketing or data mining, and never sell it.

## Why we use your data, and on what grounds

Everything we receive comes from you, from your phone, or from the stores and RevenueCat as described above. The stores also show us download numbers and, from phones whose owners chose to share diagnostics with app developers, crash reports without names or email addresses. We get nothing else about you from other companies, and we use what we receive only for the purposes below. The laws of many countries, such as the GDPR, ask us to name the legal ground for each.

Running the cloud backup and signing you in, checking and recording your RepQuest Pro subscription, and downloading the map around you while you track an activity with the real map on: because it is needed to provide what you asked for under our terms (contract).

Backing up the data above that can count as health data: your explicit consent, which you give by ticking the box that names this data before the app sends you a sign-in code. It is separate from accepting the terms, and without it the app makes no account and backs nothing up. You can withdraw it at any time by deleting your account in Settings → Account & backup, which deletes the backup, without affecting what was done before.

Remembering which phones have had free days, and checking the phone’s clock: our legitimate interest in offering free days fairly, once per phone, and preventing abuse of the free offer. The code is one-way and holds nothing else, so the impact on you is small, and it does not override your rights.

Keeping RepQuest secure and up to date, preventing abuse, fixing problems, answering your messages and defending legal claims: our legitimate interest in running a safe, working service, which does not override your rights. Keeping records the law requires, such as for tax, and answering lawful requests from authorities: a legal obligation.

You do not have to give us any personal data to use RepQuest. An email address is needed only for cloud backup, and a store account only to buy RepQuest Pro. The app makes no automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects, and it does not profile you: the end of the free days is a fixed rule, once per phone, worked out on your phone. We do not use your data to train artificial intelligence models.

We are in the Philippines, and we reach the cloud backup and our providers’ records from there to run and support RepQuest. Our cloud database is in the European Union (Ireland), and RevenueCat and Expo are in the United States. Supabase, RevenueCat and Google may also handle data in other countries, including the United States, to run their services. These countries’ data protection laws may differ from yours.

Where the law requires a safeguard for these transfers, such as for data from the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, we rely on the standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission (with the UK’s addendum) in Supabase’s and RevenueCat’s data processing terms. You can ask us for a copy.

## How long data is kept

On your phone: until you delete it, erase your data or remove the app. In the cloud backup: until you delete your account, or until we delete an account unused for two years, after warning you by email at least 30 days before, as the terms say. The backup’s working records (a copy of a change that clashed with another phone’s, and the list of changes already received, which keeps a change sent twice from counting twice) are deleted after 30 days. Your data is then deleted from our cloud database at once, and our provider’s records of sign-ins and requests, and the copies in its backups, are deleted as they expire, within 30 days.

At RevenueCat: a signed-in account’s purchase record is deleted when you delete the account. A record made without an account, which lets a purchase made on that phone be restored, is deleted once it has gone unused for 12 months with no subscription running. The stores keep their own records of your purchases. We keep the stores’ sales reports, and other records the law requires, such as for tax, for as long as that law requires. Sign-in code emails are deleted from our email account within 30 days, and messages from you are kept as the section on emails says.

Free-days codes: kept with their two dates until two years after the phone was last seen, then deleted. On your phone, the date its free days began and the time check are kept until you remove the app.

## How we protect your data

Data on your phone is protected by its app sandbox and lock screen, your sign-in is kept in the phone’s secure storage, and RepQuest uses no passwords. Data that leaves your phone travels encrypted (HTTPS), our providers encrypt the data they store, and each account can reach only its own records in the cloud database. Card details never reach us.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach puts your data at risk, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

## Your choices

Export all your data as a file at any time (Settings → Your data → Export all data), also while new terms wait for you to accept them: the screen that asks you leads there. Erase everything on the phone (Settings → Your data → Erase all data on this device; when signed in, sign out and remove it from this device). If you signed in, you can also delete your account and its cloud copy from Settings → Account & backup; this cannot be undone. Deleting your account also deletes its subscription record, at RevenueCat and in the cloud database, but it does not cancel a subscription: cancel that in Google Play or your Apple account.

If you no longer have the app, email us from your account’s address to have your account deleted; we will delete it within 30 days and confirm. To have a purchase record made without an account deleted sooner, write to us with the order number on your store receipt.

You can turn off location, motion, camera and notification access in your phone’s settings at any time, and the rest of the app keeps working. You can turn off the real map, so that no map is downloaded, in Settings → Workouts. Erasing your data does not clear the error log (clear it in Settings → Help & tutorials), the random identifier RevenueCat knows the phone by, so that a purchase made on it keeps working, the random identifier Expo’s update check uses, or when the phone’s free days began, as they come once per phone. Because the free-days code is not tied to you, we cannot find yours from your name or email address; it is deleted two years after your phone was last seen.

## Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us what personal data we hold about you, and ask us to give you a copy, to correct it, to delete it, to send it to you in a form you can take elsewhere, or to stop or limit using it, and you can withdraw any consent you gave. Much of this you can do yourself in the app (see Your choices). For the rest, write to mujarstudios@gmail.com. We may ask you to confirm the request from your account’s email address, or for something that shows the data is yours.

You also have the right to object, at any time, to our use of your data based on our legitimate interests.

Asking is free, unless a request is clearly unfounded or excessive, when the law lets us charge a reasonable fee or refuse. We answer within one month, or within the shorter time your law sets. Where the law allows, we may take up to two more months for a complex or repeated request, and will tell you why. We may keep data the law requires us to keep, or that a legal claim needs, and will tell you if we do.

Data that is only on your phone is in your hands: we cannot see, export or delete it for you.

To complain about how we handled your data, write to mujarstudios@gmail.com with “Privacy complaint” in the subject. We acknowledge it within 30 days, look into it and tell you the outcome without undue delay. You can also complain at any time, without coming to us first, to a data protection authority, such as the National Privacy Commission in the Philippines, the authority where you live or work in the EU or the EEA, the Information Commissioner’s Office in the UK, or the authority in your own country. (India’s law asks you to come to us first.)

In the United States, we do not sell your personal data, share it for cross-context behavioural advertising or use it for targeted ads. Neither the app nor any company working for us tracks you across other apps or websites, so there is no Do Not Track signal to answer. If your state gives you privacy rights, as California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut and others do, you can use them as described above, and you will not be treated differently for using them. In Brazil (the LGPD), Canada, Quebec, Australia, India, Japan, South Korea and elsewhere, you have the rights your law gives, and can complain to its authority, such as Brazil’s ANPD, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, Quebec’s Commission d’accès à l’information, Australia’s OAIC, India’s Data Protection Board, or the Personal Information Protection Commission in Japan or South Korea. Our privacy contact at mujarstudios@gmail.com is also our contact under those laws, such as Brazil’s encarregado, Quebec’s person in charge of personal information, India’s grievance officer and Korea’s chief privacy officer.

## Consumer health data in the US

Washington’s My Health My Data Act, Nevada’s similar law and others protect “consumer health data”. In RepQuest that can include your workouts, body weight, height, steps, goals and how experienced you are. We collect it from you and your phone’s sensors, only to run RepQuest. It leaves your phone only if you back it up, when it goes only to Supabase, which stores it for us, or if you share it yourself in a workout image or export. We never sell it and share it with no one else unless the law requires it.

You can confirm whether we hold it, see it, delete it and withdraw your consent to its backup, as Your rights and Your choices describe. When you ask what we hold, we also tell you every company we shared it with and how to contact it. We answer within 45 days. If we refuse a request, you can appeal by replying to our answer; we answer an appeal within 45 days and, if we refuse it, tell you how to complain to your state’s attorney general (in Washington, at atg.wa.gov).

## Children

RepQuest is not directed at children under 13, and the terms do not allow them to use it. Anyone under 18 needs a parent’s or guardian’s agreement to use it, and to create an account. If we learn that we hold any personal data of a child under 13, or of a child who made an account without the agreement their law requires, we will delete it, including a backup, a purchase record and emails.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be in the app and on its web page, with a new date. If a change is significant, such as a new use of your data or a new kind of provider, we will tell you in the app or by email before it applies, and ask for your consent where the law requires it.
